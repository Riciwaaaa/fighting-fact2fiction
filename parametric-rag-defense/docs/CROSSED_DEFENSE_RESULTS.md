# Stage C on the full 1% crossed attacker-victim matrix

Status: complete and audited on development; the predeclared practical gate passes for GLM 5.2
and Llama 3.1 70B, but the gain over model-only is not statistically resolved and does not survive
the strongest attacker in this pool. The original locked test remains unopened.

## Question and protocol

This experiment asks whether the already selected Stage C workflow generalizes when poison is
generated by a model other than the RAG victim. It uses the 61 claims that are clean-correct for
all three victims, all nine attacker-victim cells at Fact2Fiction's 1% injection rate, and 100 clean
claims per victim.

For every row, the victim model also supplies the repeated closed-book endpoint, router,
retrieval-isolated proposition check, and final endpoint selector. The poison generator is never
shown to the workflow. The final selector can copy only RAG or model-only; it cannot synthesize a
third verdict. The three attack conditions and attacker identities are private metadata joined only
for offline evaluation.

The frozen per-victim gate aggregates the three equally sized attacker cells (183 rows): Stage C
must be strictly more accurate than both endpoints, lose no more than two percentage points from
the stronger clean endpoint, and pass for at least two victims. Since each claim appears under
three attackers, uncertainty is clustered by the 61 claim IDs.

## Coverage and audit

- Stage 2: 849/849 attacker-hidden packets: 300 clean and 549 attacked.
- Router: 849/849 outputs; 325 `choose_memory`, 253 `choose_retrieval`, and 271
  `verify_proposition` routes.
- Stage C: the expected 357/357 RAG-memory disagreements received a proposition check and final
  endpoint selection; 220 selected memory and 137 selected retrieval.
- Identity/privacy audits: zero missing output, contract mismatch, wrong-model call, endpoint-copy
  violation, or visible attacker/condition/gold metadata.
- The 849 router rows map to 843 unique cached calls, and the 357 Stage C rows map to 325 unique
  checks and 356 unique selectors. These duplicates arise when attacker cells produce identical
  inference-visible packets; content-addressed reuse is expected and does not reveal attacker
  identity.

Two operational events are retained rather than hidden. The first v1 router launch stopped before
any provider call because crossed tasks used `victim_model_id` while the unchanged runner expected
`model_id`; v2 uses a content-addressed adapter that retains the immutable source key. Later, one
GLM router response repeatedly misspelled `decisive_conflict`. Before any gold or aggregate result
was read, the parser contract was versioned to repair exactly one missing/extra field pair only at
Levenshtein distance at most two, without changing field values. One Qwen proposition-check request
also timed out; an idempotent replay made only that missing call and its dependent selector.

## Primary per-victim result

Attack metrics aggregate three generators, so each row has 183 examples. “Delta” is Stage C minus
the stronger aggregate endpoint.

| Victim | Poisoned RAG | Model-only | Stage C | Endpoint oracle | Delta | Clean RAG / model-only / Stage C | Gate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| GLM 5.2 | 124/183 (67.8%) | 153/183 (83.6%) | **157/183 (85.8%)** | 172/183 (94.0%) | +4 | 82 / 80 / **85** of 100 | **Pass** |
| Llama 3.1 70B | 98/183 (53.6%) | 120/183 (65.6%) | **127/183 (69.4%)** | 142/183 (77.6%) | +7 | **72** / 54 / 70 of 100 | **Pass** |
| Qwen 3.5 35B | 98/183 (53.6%) | **144/183 (78.7%)** | 143/183 (78.1%) | 161/183 (88.0%) | -1 | **73** / **73** / 69 of 100 | Fail |

Thus the predeclared two-victim practical gate passes. Across all victims, Stage C has 427/549
(77.8%), versus model-only 417/549 (76.0%) and poisoned RAG 320/549 (58.3%). This pooled number is
descriptive: victims and attacker repetitions are not independent units.

The key uncertainty is material. Claim-clustered Stage-C-minus-model-only deltas are:

| Victim | Observed delta | Claim-clustered percentile 95% interval | Exact paired p-value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GLM 5.2 | +2.19 points | [-3.83, +8.74] | 0.503 |
| Llama 3.1 70B | +3.83 points | [-3.83, +10.93] | 0.281 |
| Qwen 3.5 35B | -0.55 points | [-3.83, +2.73] | 1.000 |

The corresponding gains over poisoned RAG are large and their clustered intervals exclude zero,
but poisoned RAG is not the hard baseline. With only 61 independent claim clusters, this run gives
a useful performance signal, not a statistically secure model-general win over closed-book.

## All nine cells

Rows are poison generators. Each cell reports `RAG / model-only / Stage C / oracle`, all out of 61.

| Attacker → / Victim ↓ | GLM 5.2 | Llama 3.1 70B | Qwen 3.5 35B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **GLM 5.2 poison** | 28 / **51** / 46 / 55 | 22 / **40** / 37 / 44 | 22 / **48** / **48** / 51 |
| **Llama poison** | 54 / 51 / **56** / 60 | 47 / 40 / **50** / 54 | **49** / 48 / **49** / 58 |
| **Qwen poison** | 42 / 51 / **55** / 57 | 29 / **40** / **40** / 44 | 27 / **48** / 46 / 52 |

The workflow does not beat model-only under GLM poison: aggregated across victims it reaches
131/183 versus model-only 139/183. It wins under the weak Llama attacker (155 versus the stronger
RAG endpoint's 150) and narrowly under Qwen poison (141 versus model-only 139). Consequently, the
positive per-victim aggregate is not evidence of strong-attacker robustness; most of its net gain
comes from the weakest generator.

The failure pattern is specific. Under GLM poison, Stage C recovers seven retrieval-only cases but
sacrifices fifteen model-only cases. Its isolated fact check is often useful, yet the final selector
still returns to a coherent poisoned endpoint too often. Qwen also loses four clean points even
though its preceding router reaches 77/100 clean, showing that unconditional Stage C on every
endpoint disagreement can itself become an information bottleneck.

## Defensible workshop claim

The strongest claim supported by this development experiment is:

> Across three non-adaptive 1% poison generators, an attacker-blind same-model workflow can obtain
> a descriptive aggregate improvement over both poisoned RAG and repeated closed-book for two of
> three victim models while meeting a small clean-utility budget.

The paper must immediately qualify that claim: the improvements over model-only have wide
claim-clustered intervals, Qwen fails, and the workflow does not outperform model-only against the
strongest generator. This is broader and more credible than the original diagonal-only result, but
it is still workshop-level development evidence rather than a confirmed robust defense.

## Recommended next method iteration

Do not tune another endpoint selector directly on these 61 labeled claims. Use the present run for
failure analysis, then freeze a new workflow before opening the locked claims:

1. **Make the verification target retrieval-independent.** Decompose the original claim into
   neutral atomic propositions before either endpoint is shown. The current router chooses a
   pivotal proposition after reading the RAG rationale, which lets coherent poison frame the check.
2. **Use a safety-first LLM workflow, not an attacker-specific rule.** On endpoint conflict, treat
   model-only as the fallback and allow RAG only when retrieval-isolated checks positively support
   its distinguishing proposition and do not support the model-only alternative. Preserve an
   explicit low-confidence/escalation state internally, with a predeclared binary fallback for
   AVeriTeC scoring.
3. **Add a counter-check with a fixed budget.** Ask the same model once for evidence supporting the
   retrieval-specific proposition and once for the strongest counterargument, in separate
   retrieval-free contexts. A final selector sees structured conclusions, not free-form endpoint
   rationales. Compare against two cost-matched direct end-claim calls.
4. **Keep same-model primary; add multi-model only as a factorial ablation.** Cross-model checking
   can test whether correlated parametric errors are the bottleneck, but it should not replace the
   same-model headline or obscure compute/model advantages.
5. **Evaluate attacker-held-out generalization.** Add more poison generators or seeds, select the
   workflow without using one generator's outcomes, and report that held-out attacker plus the
   untouched locked claims. The success criterion should require improvement over model-only with
   a claim-clustered interval or a larger powered sample, not only a positive point estimate.

The first offline diagnostic for this iteration should measure whether the current proposition
check, router endpoint, and final endpoint are calibrated separately under each attacker. If the
isolated check is correct but the final selector reverses it under GLM poison, the bottleneck is the
selector packet. If the isolated check itself follows poison-framed propositions, neutral
pre-decomposition is the required change.
